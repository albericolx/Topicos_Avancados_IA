\begin{abstract}
The unchecked proliferation of clandestine online gambling platforms (\textit{bets}) has established a severe socioeconomic crisis in Brazil, siphoning between R\$~6 and R\$~10 billion monthly through evasive digital infrastructure, dynamic payment gateways (Pix), ephemeral mirror domains, and polymorphic Android Application Packages (APKs). Conventional security countermeasures---such as static URL blacklists, keyboard filters, and centralized cloud moderation---fail systematically against Domain Generation Algorithms (DGA) and automated mass malware factories, while compromising user privacy. To address this critical gap, this paper introduces \textbf{Sentinela Clandestino}, an edge-native, two-stage hybrid AI architecture for real-time threat containment. Operating strictly within the smartphone without cloud telemetry, our system captures external redirect intents and APK transfers at the operating system boundary. A lightweight, open-weights System~1 decision model (\textit{LAYA}) performs sub-35ms probabilistic risk classification with a footprint below 100\,MB of RAM, while a quantized 4-bit Small Language Model (SLM, 1B--3B) is triggered on-demand to audit complex predatory withdrawal clauses and bytecode discrepancies, orchestrated alongside local passive DNS graph summaries via the Model Context Protocol (MCP). Rather than enforcing paternalistic censorship, the framework channels the user's selfish economic interest by exposing transparent evidence of withdrawal freeze scams (\textit{honeypots}) and banking Trojans before deposit completion. We formalize the adversary threat model, evaluate trade-offs against ten commercial tools and ten state-of-the-art papers, and demonstrate how this hybrid approach reconciles deep semantic contract reasoning with imperceptible mobile navigation latency.

\keywords{Clandestine Online Gambling \and On-Device AI \and Mobile Security \and Small Language Models \and System 1 Decision Models \and Passive DNS Graphs \and AI for Good.}
\end{abstract}
